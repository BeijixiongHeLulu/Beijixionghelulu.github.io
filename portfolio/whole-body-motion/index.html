Human performance during whole-body motion

What shapes directional responses during whole-body motion?

Unity task development · Response-time analysis

Manuscript submitted

At the Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences, I developed a Unity directional-judgment task for an existing six-degree-of-freedom motion platform and analyzed response times.

Participants responded to an arrow using the corresponding directional key. Practice introduced the mapping with feedback; formal trials provided the behavioral records.

Commands and measurements

Commanding motion and measuring motion answer different questions. Unity specified the platform’s motion conditions, while sensors beneath the seat recorded the movement that occurred. Keeping these roles separate allowed the study to relate task performance to measured motion rather than treating a command setting as the measurement itself.

Task implementation

I implemented cue presentation and directional-response capture in Unity. The task emitted cue and response events alongside behavioral records, providing reference points for relating responses to the independent motion recordings.

How motion was related to each cue

For each cue, motion was summarized over a fixed preceding interval and expressed relative to the direction of that cue. The window did not depend on how quickly the participant responded. This linked the definition of the behavioral task to the way motion was represented in the analysis.

Community evaluations and evidence accumulation

How are community evaluations reflected in decisions?

Second author · Data analysis

Manuscript submitted

As a research assistant at Lifedu, I carried out the data analyses for a collaborative study combining an implicit association task, electroencephalography (EEG) and decision modeling.

Participants classified community images and positive or negative words. “Own” meant their residential community; “other” meant another community. Across two response mappings, the community category sharing a key with positive words changed.

Connecting measurements and models

I preprocessed the EEG recordings and extracted slopes of centroparietal positivity (CPP), then matched valid EEG measures with the corresponding behavioral trials.

I fitted drift diffusion models to response times and correct or incorrect choices. Drift rate represents the model’s rate of evidence accumulation. In joint EEG–behavior models, trial-level CPP slopes entered the drift-rate specification.

Keeping valid error responses

In this study, error trials were excluded from response-time summaries, while valid error responses were retained as choice information for decision modeling. The model needed the pattern of correct and incorrect responses as well as their timing.

Model fitting and comparison

Models were fitted using aggregate maximum-likelihood estimation in PyDDM. I compared candidate specifications using BIC, considering fit alongside model complexity. CPP slope entered the drift-rate specification as a measured covariate; the resulting drift rate was a model parameter.

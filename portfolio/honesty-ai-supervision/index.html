Honesty under AI supervision

How do supervision and incentives shape honest reporting? In four behavioral experiments, participants predicted a computer-presented coin outcome and then reported whether they had guessed correctly. The experiments used programmed supervision and feedback conditions framed as AI oversight.

Co-first author · Experimental design and analysis

Published 2026

My contributions included experimental design, PsychoPy task development, behavioral data collection, and analysis in Python and R.

Study design

The task recorded the prediction and outcome, allowing actual correctness to be compared with the participant’s report. Claiming a correct prediction earned more task tokens.

Experiments 1 and 2 examined visible and uncertain monitoring sequentially in the same participants. Experiments 3a and 3b examined monetary rewards and moral feedback in separate groups. Personality traits were measured with the Short Dark Triad (SD3) scale and examined alongside the intervention conditions.

Behavior after reward withdrawal

In the monetary-reward experiment, extra rewards for consistent honesty were introduced and later removed without announcing the rule. The comparison separated behavior during reward delivery from persistence after withdrawal within the task.

Published findings

Honest reporting was higher when monitoring was visible in this task. Monetary rewards produced a clearer increase in honest reporting than moral feedback, with behavior also varying across measured traits and experimental phases.

Trial-level analysis

One analysis modeled trial-level honesty using supervision conditions, personality scores and their interactions, with a participant random intercept to account for repeated observations.

Publication

Neural patterns in interpersonal emotion regulation

How do two people coordinate when one helps the other regulate emotion? This study compared cognitive reappraisal and distraction while recording both participants’ emotional ratings and functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) signals.

Co-author · Data analysis

Published 2026

I contributed across the behavioral and fNIRS data-analysis workflow, including preprocessing, activation analysis, interbrain synchrony and multivariate pattern analysis.

From interaction to analysis

Participants viewed and rated an image, completed a regulation interaction, then viewed and rated the same image again. One person, the regulator, offered reappraisal or a distraction task; the other, the target, responded.

Behavioral models compared ratings before and after regulation across strategies and roles. Activation analysis estimated task-related changes in oxygenated hemoglobin and compared responses across strategies and roles.

For each trial, correlations between the two participants’ regional signals formed an interbrain-synchrony matrix. These cross-person patterns provided features for multivariate classification.

Accounting for interacting pairs

Behavioral and activation models accounted for shared variation within each dyad through a dyad random intercept. This retained the paired structure when comparing strategies and roles.

Published findings

The published analyses found role- and strategy-dependent neural responses, alongside interbrain patterns informative about regulation strategy and success.

Analysis details

Each trial produced a 10×10 matrix of Pearson correlations between the two participants’ regional time series. SVM-based classification evaluated strategy and role-specific success using five-fold cross-validation within participants. Preprocessing included signal-quality checks, motion-artifact handling and conversion from optical measurements to hemoglobin changes.
